Empirical Research & Publications
Core Finding
"India's CPI Granger-causes Singapore's inflation with a two-month lag (p = 0.028). Singapore's in turn Granger-causes the UK's (p = 0.039). Neither reverse direction holds. The transmission chain runs one way: emerging market supply dynamics lead the advanced economy inflation cycle."
Published · SSRN Working Paper · Version 2 · 2026
Cross-Country Macroeconomic Dynamics: Inflation, Growth, and Monetary Policy — India, Singapore, and the United Kingdom
Gokhale, A.A. (2026) · ssrn.com/abstract=6514338
Documents asymmetric Granger causality chain India → Singapore → UK (p = 0.028). Sectoral decomposition identifies Fuel and Light and Core manufactured goods as the transmission mechanism, consistent with the Dominant Currency Paradigm. Episode-specific analysis reveals the India → Singapore link activates during supply-driven inflationary regimes. Employs VAR(2), Johansen cointegration, Markov-switching, and Diebold-Mariano forecasting.
Published · SSRN Working Paper · 2026
Measuring Core Inflation for Monetary Policy in Singapore: Replication and Extension of Roger & Xiong (2025)
Gokhale, A.A. (2026) · ssrn.com/abstract=6847179 · MAS Staff Paper No. 2025/1
Full replication of six core inflation measures from publicly available SingStat data. Four original extensions: structural break analysis (Chow test, 2021), real-time Diebold-Mariano forecasting horse race, novel India upstream contamination test, and Phillips curve augmentation. Finds XFE and T15 fail the upstream contamination check.
Published · SSRN Working Paper · 2026
Who Bears the Burden? Real Wage and Employment Adjustment to Upstream Inflation Shocks Across Monetary Policy Regimes — Singapore and the United Kingdom
Gokhale, A.A. (2026) · ssrn.com/abstract=7118139
Examines real wage and employment adjustment across monetary policy regimes during the 2022 supply shock. Singapore's S$NEER exchange rate framework preserved real wages (+7.7% cumulative 2022–2025) while UK workers experienced two consecutive years of negative real wages. Employs difference-in-differences and synthetic control methods.
Published · SSRN Working Paper · 2026
Who Leaves When Rates Rise? Balance Sheet Heterogeneity and the Emigration Response to Monetary Tightening
Gokhale, A.A. (2026) · ssrn.com/abstract=7604979
Difference-in-differences design exploiting mortgage rate expiry timing during the 2022–23 UK tightening cycle. Finds a 2.14 percentage point increase in emigration intentions among mortgage-exposed households. Renter versus homeowner heterogeneity supports an exit-friction mechanism; formalised in a companion two-agent New Keynesian model calibrated in Julia.

Research Agenda
What I'm Working On
Interest 01
Dominant Currency Transmission
The sectoral decomposition confirms fuel and core manufactured goods drive the India → Singapore channel. The next question: does dollar invoicing mediate the two-month lag? Testing the DCP mechanism directly in the Asian corridor.
Interest 02
Monetary Policy and Firm Dynamics
Does the instrument of monetary tightening — exchange rate versus interest rate — determine which firms exit and which survive? Singapore and the UK tightened simultaneously but through different mechanisms. The micro consequences remain unexplored.
Interest 03
State-Dependent Monetary Effectiveness
Replicating Zanetti et al. (CFM-DP2026-10): does monetary policy lose traction during global supply chain disruptions? Extending the threshold VAR framework to the Asian corridor to test whether the state dependence result holds outside the Euro Area.
Interest 04
Migration and Monetary Transmission
Skilled emigration shapes the household credit channel through which monetary policy operates. The emigration paper documents balance-sheet heterogeneity across mortgage-exposed and renting households. Extensions to remittance flows and local labour market adjustment are in progress.
